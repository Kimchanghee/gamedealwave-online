"Officially licensed" sounds like the end of the compatibility question, and for a PS5 M.2 upgrade it is only part of it. For the WD_BLACK SN850P 2TB, the useful question is what the label tells you, what it leaves for you to check, and which lines on the listing should match before the slot cover goes back on.

This checklist uses Sony's PS5 M.2 support page, the WD_BLACK product page from Sandisk and the exact Amazon product page. We have not used this drive, installed it in a console or timed any game. It quotes no price, discount, stock level or customer rating, and neither source promises that every drive meeting the numbers will work.

## What does "officially licensed for PS5" actually say?
It is a statement from the maker and the listing. The Amazon page lists the special feature as Officially Licensed for Playstation 5, and the Sandisk page says the drive is officially licensed for the PlayStation 5 console and is certified and tested for PS5 consoles. The listing also notes that PS5 system software compatibility should be checked on the official WD_BLACK site.

Sony's own support page is more cautious. It says Sony cannot guarantee that all M.2 SSD devices meeting the described specifications will work with the console. Treat the licensed label as a strong sign from the maker, and treat Sony's table as the rule you still check. Our [PS5 M.2 SSD checklist](/guides/ps5-m2-ssd-checklist/) walks through that table.

## Does the drive match Sony's interface and size rules?
Sony asks for a PCI-Express Gen4 x4 M.2 NVMe SSD with a Key M socket, 250 GB to 8 TB, in a 2230, 2242, 2260, 2280 or 22110 form factor, with a sequential read speed of 5,500 MB/s or faster recommended. The Sandisk page lists the 2TB model as PCIe 4.0 x4, M.2 2280, with sequential reads up to 7,300 MB/s and writes up to 6,600 MB/s.

On those lines the two pages agree: interface, form factor, capacity and read speed all sit inside Sony's table. Sony also says M.2 SATA drives are not supported, and the Amazon page lists the interface as NVMe, so that line is covered too.

## Will the built-in heatsink fit under the slot cover?
Sony limits the whole drive including its cooling structure to 25 mm wide and 11.25 mm thick, and it warns that heatsink sizes vary greatly. Sandisk lists this drive at 3.15 by 0.96 by 0.39 inches, which works out to roughly 80 mm long, 24.4 mm wide and 9.9 mm thick. The Amazon page shows a form factor figure of 9.89 millimeters, which fits the same thickness.

Those numbers sit inside Sony's limits, but they are combined figures. Sony also splits the thickness into 8.0 mm above the board and 2.45 mm below it, and the listing does not give that split, so we cannot confirm it for you. If the cover does not close properly, Sony says the cooling structure likely exceeds the size requirements, and Sony recommends against adding a second heatsink to a drive that already has one.

## Which details on the Amazon listing should you read twice?
The page we opened is titled WD_BLACK 2TB SN850P NVMe M.2 SSD Officially Licensed Storage Expansion for PS5 Consoles, with heatsink, and it names the model WDBBYV0020BNC-WRSN. The capacity selector showed 2TB, the brand WD_BLACK and the form factor M.2 2280. It was filed under Internal Solid State Drives and showed no Currently unavailable notice when we opened it, though US availability for your address can differ.

One field is inconsistent. The detail table's manufacturer part number line shows WDBBYV0010BNC-WRSN, which looks like a 1 TB code, while the title and model number say WDBBYV0020BNC-WRSN. We used the title and model number and left the part number line out. Match the capacity on the page you order from before relying on any code.

## What happens after you install it?
Sony says a first-time M.2 SSD is formatted in the console after installation, and formatting erases everything on the drive. The whole drive must be formatted, all data for one game has to stay in one place, and the PS5 does not support Host Memory Buffer. Update the console to the latest system software before installing, touch a grounded metal object, and keep the console off while the cover is removed.

If a game gives you trouble on the new drive, Sony's advice is to move it back to the console's internal storage.

## Who should skip this drive?
Skip it if you only want somewhere to park games you are not playing. A USB drive can hold PS5 games, but they must be copied back before you can play them, as covered in our [storage aisle](/category/storage/). Skip it too if your console is the only reason you are shopping and you would rather buy a bare drive and pick your own heatsink, which is a different set of checks.

Also skip it if the model code on the page you land on does not match WDBBYV0020BNC-WRSN, or if you are not sure how the listed thickness splits above and below the board. Ask the seller or the maker first.

## What should you write down before ordering?
Write down the exact product name, the 2TB capacity, the model code WDBBYV0020BNC-WRSN and the words with heatsink. Add Sony's limits for width and thickness, the system software note from the maker and the date you read Sony's page. Open that page again on the day you order, because requirements can change with system updates.

If every line matches, the drive is a candidate for adding PS5 game space inside the console. If any line is unclear, settle it first.
