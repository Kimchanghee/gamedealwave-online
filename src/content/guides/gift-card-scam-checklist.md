Gaming gift cards are convenient, which is exactly why scammers like them: once a code is redeemed, the money is very hard to recover. The checks below separate a normal purchase from the patterns that consumer agencies warn about. They come from the US Federal Trade Commission's gift card guidance and platform support pages.

## Who asks to be paid with a gift card?

The FTC's rule is blunt: **anyone who demands payment by gift card is a scammer**. Legitimate businesses and government agencies do not ask you to pay bills, fines, fees or "account unlock" charges with gift cards. In gaming, common versions are:

- A "support agent" who says your account will be banned unless you pay with store cards.
- A trader offering rare in-game items if you send gift card codes first.
- A "giveaway" that asks for a card code to verify your account.

If someone wants the numbers on the back of a card, stop. Platform support never needs your unredeemed code to help you.

## How do you check a physical card in a store?

The FTC suggests inspecting cards before you buy them. Look for packaging that has been opened or resealed, and a PIN area that has already been scratched or looks tampered with. Tampered cards can be drained the moment they are activated. Pick a card from the back of the rack, check it, and keep the receipt.

## What about buying codes online?

Online codes are fine from the right seller. The risk sits with **unknown resellers offering steep discounts**. Codes sold that way can be already redeemed, region-locked to another country, or bought with stolen payment details, which can lead to the balance being removed later. Practical checks:

1. Prefer listings sold by the retailer or platform itself.
2. Read the region and platform on the listing; see the [gift card region checklist](/guides/gift-card-region-checklist/).
3. Check how the code is delivered and what happens if it does not work.
4. Be wary of sellers who ask you to complete the purchase outside the store's checkout.

## How should you give a gift card as a present?

Give the physical card or the official digital gift, and keep the receipt until the recipient confirms the balance. Do not post a photo of the card or its code on social media; anyone who reads the code can redeem it first.

## What if you have already been scammed?

The FTC's advice is to act quickly:

- Contact the company that issued the card and tell them it was used in a scam; ask whether the money can be frozen or refunded.
- Keep the card and the receipt.
- Report it at [ReportFraud.ftc.gov](https://reportfraud.ftc.gov/).

For codes that simply do not work, Xbox support recommends checking whether the code was already redeemed or is inactive, and then contacting the retailer that sold it.

## Red flags at a glance

- Payment by gift card for anything that is not a gift.
- A stranger asking for a code or a photo of the card.
- Opened packaging or a scratched PIN area.
- A price far below every major retailer.
