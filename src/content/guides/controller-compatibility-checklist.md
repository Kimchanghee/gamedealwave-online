Controllers look universal and are not. The same pad can be fully supported on one console, limited to older games on the next, and not recognized on a third. This checklist covers the compatibility questions first, then the features that justify paying more.

## Does it match your console generation?

**PlayStation.** Sony's backward compatibility page says the DualSense, the DualShock 4 and PlayStation officially licensed third-party gamepads work on PS5 when you play **PS4 games**. For **PS5 games**, you need a DualSense (or DualSense Edge) or a licensed controller that supports PS5 games. A DualShock 4 is a fine second pad for PS4 titles on a PS5, but it will not run PS5 games.

**Xbox.** Xbox Wireless Controllers are designed to work across Xbox One and Xbox Series X|S. Third-party pads are marked "Designed for Xbox" when they are licensed. Check the listing for the specific console names rather than "Xbox compatible".

**Nintendo.** Nintendo's compatibility page for Switch 2 says original Switch Joy-Con can be used **wirelessly** with Switch 2, but accessories built around the old Joy-Con shape, such as the Joy-Con grip and strap, do not fit Joy-Con 2. If you are moving from Switch to Switch 2, check each accessory against that page.

## Official, licensed, or unlicensed?

There are three tiers on any listing:

1. **First-party**, made by Sony, Microsoft or Nintendo.
2. **Officially licensed**, made by another company with the platform's approval. The box carries the platform's licensing mark.
3. **Unlicensed**, which may work today but has no guarantee after a system update and may lack features such as wake-from-sleep or headset audio.

Licensed pads are usually the safest way to save money. Unlicensed ones are the riskiest for future compatibility.

## Wired, wireless, or both?

Wireless is convenient for the couch. Wired removes battery concerns and is common on budget licensed pads, especially for PC use. Some controllers are **wired-only** even though the photos show no cable; read the connection line in the specifications. For PC, check whether the pad connects by Bluetooth, a USB dongle, or a cable.

## What powers it?

Pads use either a **built-in rechargeable battery** (DualSense, most Switch controllers) or **replaceable AA batteries or a battery pack** (the standard Xbox Wireless Controller). Built-in batteries are convenient but wear out over years; replaceable batteries let you swap mid-session. Check what is in the box: some controllers ship without batteries or without a charging cable.

## Are pro features worth paying for?

Higher-priced "pro" controllers add things such as **back paddles or buttons, adjustable trigger stops, swappable stick caps, and replaceable stick modules**. Replaceable sticks matter if you have had stick drift before, because the module can be replaced instead of the whole pad. Only pay for the features listed in the specifications, and confirm the console support for those features, since some work only in certain modes.

## Mistakes to avoid

- Buying a DualShock 4 as your only controller for PS5 games.
- Assuming a pad is wireless because the photo shows no cable.
- Buying old Joy-Con accessories for a Switch 2.
