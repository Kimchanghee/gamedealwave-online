The white DualSense is the standard PS5 controller, and it is also a common pick for a second player or a PC pad. Sony's pages draw a clear line between what works on a console and what carries over to other devices. This checklist reads those pages for one exact listing, the white PlayStation DualSense Wireless Controller, and asks which features survive on a PC, a Mac and a phone.

It uses Sony's support page, Sony's product page, an Apple support page and the Amazon product page. We have not used this controller, paired it with any device or played a game with it. It quotes no price, discount, stock level or customer rating, and it makes no comparison with other brands.

## Which devices can connect to a DualSense?
Sony's support page lists the minimum operating systems that support Bluetooth and USB connections: Windows 10 (64 bit), Windows 11, macOS Big Sur 11.3, iOS 14.5, iPadOS 14.5, tvOS 14.5 and Android 12. Anything older than those versions is outside Sony's list, so check your device's system version before you count on the pad.

Apple's own support page agrees for its devices. It names the PS5 DualSense wireless controller among supported PlayStation controllers and says it automatically pairs when plugged directly into an Apple device with a USB cable that supports data and charging. If a controller does not pair, Apple suggests updating the controller and the Apple device, then trying Bluetooth instead.

## Which features work on PC, Mac and mobile?
Sony says that depending on the device, certain features might not be available. Its support page gives four specific notes:

- Adaptive triggers work when the game supports them, but are not compatible with Android-based mobile devices.
- Haptic feedback works when the game supports it and requires a USB connection on PC. It is not compatible with Mac computers and mobile devices.
- The built-in microphone and speaker work on Windows PC when the game supports them. They are not compatible with Mac and mobile devices.
- The headset jack requires a wired connection on PC and Mac. It is not compatible with mobile devices.

Sony's product page repeats the idea in a footnote: haptic feedback and adaptive triggers may require a USB connection and are available when supported by the game. In short, a Windows PC with a cable keeps the most features, and a phone keeps the fewest.

## How do you pair it, and can one controller keep several pairings?
For the first Bluetooth pairing, Sony says to make sure the player indicator is off, then hold the create button and the PS button until the light bar blinks. Enable Bluetooth on your device and pick the controller from the list. Each time you change devices, you must pair the controller with the new device.

A single controller can keep up to four pairings, and each one sits in a numbered slot. Sony describes a button combination, held for several seconds, to assign a slot, and says that pressing the PS button while the controller is off reconnects it to the device you used last. This helps if you move between a console and a laptop, though we have not tried the steps ourselves.

## What should you know about updates and the cable?
Sony says you can update the controller's software on a Windows PC with the free PlayStation Accessories app. Its product page also says to keep the PS5 system software and the controller software current, and that firmware updates are released from time to time.

The same page says the cable is not included. It tells buyers to use the USB cable supplied with the PS5 console to connect or charge the controller. If you plan to use the pad on a computer and the console cable stays plugged in at home, you need a data-capable USB-C cable of your own. Our [controller compatibility guide](/guides/controller-compatibility-checklist/) explains how to read the platform lines on pads before you order.

## What should the Amazon listing say?
The page we opened is titled PlayStation DualSense Wireless Controller - White, filed under Video Games, then PlayStation 5, then Accessories, then Controllers. The feature list mentions haptic feedback and adaptive triggers, a built-in microphone, a 3.5 mm headset jack, a USB Type-C or Bluetooth connection, and use on Windows PC and Mac computers.

If the listing you open shows another color, a bundle, a limited edition or a platform line other than PlayStation 5, read it fresh. Sony's own page lists many colors and special editions, and the same product name can appear on several variations.

## Who should skip this controller?
Skip it if you want back buttons, remappable controls and tunable triggers, since Sony sells the DualSense Edge for that. Our [DualSense Edge checklist](/picks/dualsense-edge-buying-checklist/) covers that question.

Skip it too if your main device is a phone and you care about haptics or adaptive triggers, because Sony lists haptics as unsupported on mobile devices and triggers as unsupported on Android. And skip it if your computer runs an operating system older than the versions Sony lists.

## What should you write down before ordering?
Write down the exact product name, the white color, the PlayStation 5 platform line, and the feature limits for your device: haptics need USB on PC, mic and speaker are Windows-only, and the headset jack needs a wired link on PC and Mac.

If all of those fit how you plan to play, the listing is a reasonable candidate. If any one is missing, read that page fresh before ordering. For other add-ons, see our [console storage aisle](/category/storage/).
