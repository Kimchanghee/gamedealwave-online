A controller title that lists many platforms can still leave the one you own off the list. The black GameSir Cyclone 2 is described as a PC controller, yet it is also sold for consoles, and the exact list matters before you order.

We have not used this controller, tested its sticks or checked it on any console. The checks below come from GameSir's product and FAQ pages and from separately attributed reviews by CNET and Impulse Gamer. No customer review, rating or product price is used here.

## Does the Cyclone 2 work with Xbox and PlayStation consoles?
Not directly. GameSir's spec lists compatible platforms as Switch, Android, iOS, PC and Steam. CNET states plainly that there is no official Xbox or PlayStation support. Its reviewer did stream from both consoles to a PC and played with the controller there, and mentioned that a remapping tool would be needed for PlayStation button layouts.

That is a PC workaround, not console support, and we have not repeated it. If your goal is to plug a pad straight into a console, check the platform holder's own accessory rules first. Our [controller compatibility checklist](/guides/controller-compatibility-checklist/) walks through that comparison in a simple order.

## What is unclear about Nintendo Switch 2?
The Amazon title for this listing mentions Switch and Switch 2. The GameSir spec pages we read list Switch, with no explicit Switch 2 wording. CNET's review refers to Nintendo Switch support and also notes that the one thing keeping it from being a great Switch controller is not the platform but its buttons. A name on a store title is not a maker compatibility statement.

Before ordering for a Switch 2, look for a current GameSir compatibility statement that names the console. If you cannot find one, treat Switch 2 use as unconfirmed. This guide takes no position on whether it works, since we have not tried it.

## What do the connection options, triggers and sticks add?
GameSir lists three connection types: Bluetooth, wired and a wireless dongle. It describes Mag-Res TMR sticks and Hall Effect analog triggers that can be switched to micro switch triggers, a 6-axis gyroscope, asymmetric rumble motors in each grip, back buttons, an 860 mAh battery and GameSir Connect software. GameSir's launch post says it supports a 1000 Hz polling rate in the wired and dongle modes.

Impulse Gamer found the sticks accurate with little resistance and no way to change it. CNET found the triggers nice and the paddles responsive, but said the face buttons and D-pad felt mushy. Those are two reviewers' impressions of specific parts, not conclusions we reached. If you play fast action games, decide which of those parts you care about most.

## What does the charging dock change?
The Amazon title says the listing includes a charging dock, and the option shown here is Black. CNET reports that lifting the controller off the stand turns it on and reconnects it to the last device, which it called its favorite feature. GameSir also sells a standard edition without the station, so confirm that the selected listing is the one that names the dock.

Our [game editions checklist](/guides/game-editions-checklist/) is a reminder that edition names matter as much for accessories as for games. Check the color, the dock wording and the platform line together before you order. We have not charged or docked the controller here, so the wake-on-lift behavior is a reviewer's report, not our observation, and it may depend on the connection mode you use.

The listing also offers a White option, so confirm that Black is the selected color if that is what you want. A different color can change which listing and which edition you land on, even when the product name looks the same.

## Who should skip the GameSir Cyclone 2?
Skip it if you want a controller with official Xbox or PlayStation console support, if you need confirmed Switch 2 compatibility from the maker, or if you are sensitive to soft face buttons. CNET also called GameSir Connect crowded and said it would recommend other controllers instead, which is a view worth weighing against the dock and the trigger options.

The useful conclusion is modest. This is a PC, Android, iOS and Switch controller with a dock option, not a confirmed console controller for every platform. Choose it for that exact set of uses, and treat Xbox, PlayStation and Switch 2 as separate questions to verify before spending. If you only play on a PC, most of the unclear points above do not apply to you, and the decision comes down to the buttons, the dock and the software that reviewers described.
