A bare M.2 drive and the same drive with a heatsink can sit side by side on one Amazon listing, and the PS5 expansion slot is picky about which one you pick. For the Samsung 990 PRO 2TB with Heatsink, the useful question is not how fast it can run. It is whether the exact variant matches what Sony asks for before the slot cover goes back on.

This checklist uses Sony's PS5 M.2 support page, Samsung's own product page and the exact Amazon product page. We have not used this drive, installed it in a console, or timed any game. It quotes no price, discount, stock level or customer rating, and neither source promises that every drive meeting the numbers will work.

## Is the Samsung 990 PRO with Heatsink listed for the PS5?
Yes, Samsung's product page names PlayStation 5 and Sony's requirement table is the one to compare it against. Samsung describes a slim heatsink and says the thin body is compatible with PlayStation 5, desktops and laptops that meet the PCI-SIG D8 standard. The Amazon listing for this variant also names PlayStation 5 in its compatible devices.

That is a maker statement, not a guarantee from Sony. Sony's own page says it cannot guarantee that every M.2 SSD meeting the described specifications will work with the console. Treat Samsung's wording as a strong sign and Sony's table as the rule you check yourself.

## Which Sony requirements should you match?
Sony asks for a PCI-Express Gen4 x4 M.2 NVMe SSD with a Key M socket, in 250 GB to 8 TB, in a 2230, 2242, 2260, 2280 or 22110 form factor. It recommends a sequential read speed of 5,500 MB/s or faster. Samsung lists this drive as PCIe Gen 4.0 x4, NVMe 2.0, M.2 2280, with sequential reads up to 7,450 MB/s and sequential writes up to 6,900 MB/s.

On those lines the two pages agree: interface, size type and capacity all sit inside Sony's table, and the read speed is above Sony's recommendation. The step-by-step comparison is in the [PS5 M.2 SSD checklist](/guides/ps5-m2-ssd-checklist/), which you can keep open while you check the listing.

## Does the built-in heatsink fit the slot?
Sony limits the whole drive including its cooling to 25 mm wide and 11.25 mm thick, with no more than 8.0 mm above the board and 2.45 mm below it. Sony also says heatsink sizes vary greatly and that you should look for another option if you are not sure one fits.

This is the line we could not settle for you. Samsung's global specification page and its Australian page list different dimensions for this model, so we left those numbers out. Check the dimension line on the Samsung page you trust, compare it with Sony's limits, and ask Samsung or the seller if the two do not clearly agree. Sony also recommends against adding a second heatsink to a drive that already has one built in.

## Which details on the Amazon listing should you read twice?
Read the style and capacity selectors first. The page we checked showed the style 990 PRO w/ Heatsink and the capacity 2TB, with the model code MZ-V9P2T0CW in the title. A sibling listing of the same family without the heatsink exists, so the style selector is the line that decides what arrives.

One more detail to read carefully: the listing's technical table shows a hard disk form factor of 2.5 inches, while the title and Samsung's page say M.2 2280. Sony's size column uses the M.2 type numbers, so use the title and Samsung's page for the form factor and keep the table value out of your decision. The page was filed under Internal Solid State Drives and showed purchase controls without a Currently unavailable notice when we opened it, but US availability for your delivery address can differ.

## What happens after you install it?
Sony says a first-time M.2 SSD is formatted in the console after installation, and formatting erases everything on the drive. The whole drive must be formatted, and all data for one game has to stay in one place, either console storage or the M.2 drive. Sony also notes that the PS5 does not support Host Memory Buffer and that some games may not behave exactly like they do on the internal storage.

Before you install, update the console to the latest system software, touch a grounded metal object and keep the console powered off with the cover removed. If a game gives you trouble, Sony's own advice is to move it back to console storage.

## Who should skip this drive?
Skip it if you only want a place to park PS5 games you are not playing. A USB drive can store them, though they must be copied back before you can play them, as described in the [PS5 USB storage guidance on our storage aisle](/category/storage/). Skip it too if the heatsink dimension on the page you trust is unclear, because a cooling structure that does not fit the slot is a return, not a fix.

Also skip it if your console is the only reason you are shopping and you would rather buy a bare drive plus a separate heatsink of your choice. That is a different product with different checks, and this post does not cover it.

## What should you write down before ordering?
Write down the exact product name, the style, the capacity and the model code MZ-V9P2T0CW. Add the Sony limits for width and thickness, the dimension line from the Samsung page you read, and your console's model group so you know which cover steps apply. Then open the Sony support page again on the day you order, because requirements can change with system updates.

If every line matches, this drive is a candidate for adding PS5 game space inside the console. If any line is unclear, settle it with the seller or Samsung first. The goal is a drive that fits the slot and the cover on the first try, not one that only looks right in a spec table.
